# Lantern Liturgy

Darkness is the canvas and light is the only ink. Every image begins as night: deep indigo
shadows, a held breath of violet haze. Light is never spread evenly. It is spent like gold leaf,
and only where the eye should rest: a brazier's heart, a crown's points, the rim of a
sigil. What the light does not touch falls back into dusk. Restraint does the work here.

Colour is a duet, never a chorus. Cold violet and indigo hold the shadows; warm gold and ember
hold the highlights; ivory is kept for the few words. There are no other hues. The split between
cold and warm gives every frame its depth, and the grade is calibrated until the pair reads as
one material, like candlelight on old vellum.

Magic is shown as matter, not as effect: motes that drift with weight, glints with four points
like struck metal, rays that fall through the air because the air is thick. Particles gather where
light gathers and thin where it fades. Nothing sparkles at random. Every mote is placed as though
by patient hands, as a master illuminator would gild a single capital over a long evening.

The frame is a stage. Cinematic bars hold the image like a reliquary holds a relic. Within them the
composition breathes: one subject, one light, one line of text. Type is engraved rather than
printed: small capitals widely spaced, a thin rule, an italic whisper beneath. It never shouts and
never crowds the image.

Craft is the whole argument. Each piece should look labored over: the bloom measured, the grain
fine as film, the vignette felt rather than seen. These are images by someone at the top of their
field, who knows when to stop.
